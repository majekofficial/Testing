Levers, pulleys and ramps: trading force for distance

A simple machine does not create energy. It changes the size or direction of a force so that a job becomes easier, usually by making you move through a longer distance. Three numbers describe how well it works: the mechanical advantage, the velocity ratio and the efficiency.

The three quantities

mechanical advantage MA = load ÷ effort

velocity ratio VR = distance moved by effort ÷ distance moved by load

efficiency η = (MA ÷ VR) × 100

  • Load is the force the machine overcomes, such as the weight being lifted. Effort is the force you apply.
  • MA compares forces: how many times the machine multiplies your effort.
  • VR compares distances and depends only on the machine's geometry. For a pulley system it equals the number of rope sections supporting the load.
  • Efficiency is below 100% in real machines because friction and the weight of moving parts waste some energy.

Worked examples

Mechanical advantage

A load of 600 N is lifted with an effort of 150 N: MA = 600 ÷ 150 = 4. The machine multiplies your force by four. If you know MA = 4 and the load is 600 N, the effort needed is 600 ÷ 4 = 150 N.

Velocity ratio

To raise a load by 0.5 m you pull 2 m of rope: VR = 2 ÷ 0.5 = 4. This matches a pulley system with four supporting rope sections.

Efficiency

A machine with MA = 4 and VR = 5 has efficiency (4 ÷ 5) × 100 = 80%. A perfect machine would have MA equal to VR. Here friction reduces the actual advantage to 4 instead of 5.

Why you never get something for nothing

The work you put in equals the work you get out, plus losses. A machine that quadruples your force makes you pull four times as far. If the ideal effort for a 600 N load with VR = 4 is 150 N and you pull 2 m, you do 300 J of work to lift the load 0.5 m, which needs 300 J. In practice the effort is slightly higher because of friction, and the efficiency tells you by how much.

Common machines

  • Lever: VR is the ratio of the arm lengths, effort arm divided by load arm.
  • Pulley system: VR is the number of rope sections supporting the load.
  • Inclined plane: VR is the length of the slope divided by its height.
  • Wheel and axle, gears and screws follow the same logic, with VR set by radii, teeth or thread pitch.

Common mistakes

  • Swapping load and effort, which gives a mechanical advantage that is the reciprocal of the right value.
  • Expecting MA to exceed VR. If it seems to, check your measurements.
  • Measuring distances along the wrong path. Use the distance each force actually moves.
  • Forgetting that a machine can change direction without multiplying force, which gives MA = 1.

Frequently asked questions

Can mechanical advantage be less than 1?

Yes. A machine such as a fishing rod gives a gain in distance and speed instead of force, so its MA is below 1.

Why is efficiency never 100%?

Friction and the weight of moving parts convert some of the input energy into heat.

What does velocity ratio measure?

How far the effort moves compared with the load. It is set by the design of the machine.
