Working out how much a discount really saves

A sale sign shows a percentage; your wallet cares about the price. Enter the original price and the discount percentage, and this calculator returns both the amount saved and the price you pay.

The formula

saving = original price × discount ÷ 100

sale price = original price − saving

The percentage is always applied to the original price. A 25% discount on 80 saves 20, leaving 60. Equivalent shortcut: multiply the original price by 1 minus the discount as a decimal, so 80 × 0.75 = 60.

Worked examples

A clean percentage

80 with 25% off.

80 × 25 ÷ 100 = 20 saved, 80 − 20 = 60 to pay

Prices with cents

49.99 with 15% off. The saving is 7.4985, which the calculator rounds and shows as 7.5, and the price is 42.49.

A full discount

A 100% discount leaves 0 to pay.

Two discounts in a row

A 20% reduction followed by a further 10% off the new price is not 30% off. Start with 200 and apply the first discount: you save 40 and pay 160. Now apply 10% to 160: you save 16 and pay 144.

The total saving is 56 on 200, which is 28%. The second percentage applies to a smaller number.

Working backwards

If you know the sale price and the discount, you can recover the original price by hand. Divide the sale price by 1 minus the discount as a decimal. A price of 60 after a 25% discount started at 60 ÷ 0.75 = 80.

Is it a good deal?

  • Check the starting price. A discount from an inflated “original” is not a saving. Compare with other shops.
  • Add delivery and tax after the discount. Use the Tax Calculator on the sale price, or add the shipping fee yourself.
  • Compare unit prices. A 20% discount on a larger pack may still cost more per item than a smaller pack at full price.
  • Think about need. A saving only counts if you would have bought the item anyway.

Common mistakes

  • Adding stacked percentages together instead of applying them one after the other.
  • Entering the discounted price in the original price field.
  • Forgetting that the percentage is out of 100: for 0.5% enter 0.5, not 50.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate 20% off?

Multiply the price by 0.20 to find the saving and subtract it, or multiply the price by 0.80 to get the sale price directly.

Is a 50% discount the same as half price?

Yes. A 50% discount leaves 50% of the original price.

Why do the saving and sale price sometimes differ by a cent from my receipt?

Shops round at different stages. This calculator rounds the displayed figures, so a one-cent difference is normal.

Does the calculator include tax?

No. Add tax separately after the discount, as the final price in many shops includes it.
