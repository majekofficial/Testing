Comparing pay quoted per year, month or week

Job offers rarely arrive in the unit you think in. An advertisement says 48,000 a year, your rent is due monthly and a part-time shift pays by the week. This calculator takes one figure, tells it how often that amount is paid, and shows the same pay as a yearly, monthly, weekly and daily amount side by side.

How the conversion works

Choose Yearly, Monthly or Weekly for the amount you enter. The calculator converts it to a yearly figure first, then divides that figure into the other periods using fixed assumptions:

  • a year has 12 months and 52 weeks
  • a year has 260 working days (5 days a week for 52 weeks)

monthly = yearly ÷ 12 | weekly = yearly ÷ 52 | daily = yearly ÷ 260

A weekly amount is multiplied by 52 to find the year. Because 52 weeks is 364 days, and some people work fewer days because of holidays or unpaid leave, treat weekly and daily results as estimates.

Worked examples

Starting from a yearly salary

48,000 a year gives:

  • monthly: 48,000 ÷ 12 = 4,000
  • weekly: 48,000 ÷ 52 = 923.08
  • daily: 48,000 ÷ 260 = 184.62

Starting from a monthly amount

2,500 a month is 2,500 × 12 = 30,000 a year, which is 576.92 a week and 115.38 a day.

Starting from a weekly wage

600 a week is 600 × 52 = 31,200 a year, 2,600 a month and 120 a day.

Estimating an hourly rate

The calculator does not show hours, but you can finish the job by hand. If the 600 weekly wage covers 40 hours, the hourly rate is 600 ÷ 40 = 15.

Reading the result sensibly

  • Gross versus take-home. The figures are exactly what you enter, converted. Tax, pension and other deductions are not calculated, so your bank deposit will be lower.
  • Twelve or thirteen payments. Some employers pay a 13th month. If so, divide the yearly total by 13 for the payment size, not 12.
  • Currency symbol. The selector in the page header only changes the symbol shown. It does not convert between currencies, which is a job for the Currency Converter.

Common mistakes

  • Entering a monthly salary while the selector is still on Yearly. The result will be twelve times too small.
  • Comparing two jobs by their headline numbers when one pays for 48 weeks and the other for 52.
  • Using the daily figure as a pay rate for contractors, who may have unpaid days and costs.

To see how much of a monthly figure is tax, try the Tax Calculator with your own rate. To plan how much of it you can set aside, use the Savings Goal Calculator.

Frequently asked questions

How many working days are in a year?

The calculator uses 260, which is 52 weeks times 5 days. Public holidays and leave are not subtracted.

Is the monthly figure always yearly divided by 12?

Yes here. Actual pay can differ if your employer pays four-weekly, fortnightly or in 13 instalments.

Does the calculator show hourly pay?

No. Divide the weekly figure by the hours you work, as in the example above.

Does this calculate net pay?

No. It converts the amount you enter between pay periods and does not apply tax or deductions.
