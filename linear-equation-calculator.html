Solving a linear equation step by step

A linear equation has an unknown that appears only to the first power, such as 3x + 5 = 20. Type the equation, brackets, decimals and fractions included, and the calculator returns the value of x and shows the steps. A check line confirms that both sides are equal when the answer is substituted back.

The method

  1. Expand any brackets.
  2. Collect the terms containing x on one side and the numbers on the other, changing sign when a term crosses the equals sign.
  3. Combine like terms.
  4. Divide both sides by the coefficient of x.

Worked examples

A two-step equation

3x + 5 = 20. Subtract 5 to get 3x = 15, then divide by 3: x = 5. Check: 3 × 5 + 5 = 20.

Brackets and unknowns on both sides

2(x + 4) = 3x − 1. Expanding gives 2x + 8 = 3x − 1. Subtract 2x: 8 = x − 1. Add 1: x = 9.

Fractions

x/3 + 2 = 7. Subtract 2 to get x/3 = 5, then multiply both sides by 3: x = 15. For x/2 + x/3 = 5, multiply by 6 to clear the fractions: 3x + 2x = 30, so x = 6.

Decimals

0.5x − 1.5 = 4. Add 1.5 to get 0.5x = 5.5, then divide by 0.5: x = 11.

Two special cases

  • No solution. 2x + 1 = 2x + 5 reduces to 1 = 5, which is never true. The calculator reports that the two sides can never be equal.
  • Every value works. 2x + 4 = 2(x + 2) reduces to 4 = 4, which is always true. The calculator reports that every value works.

Common mistakes

  • Subtracting a term from only one side. Whatever you do to one side you must do to the other.
  • Not changing the sign of a term that moves across the equals sign.
  • Forgetting to multiply every term in a bracket by the number outside.
  • Multiplying to clear fractions but missing a term, such as multiplying x/3 by 3 but leaving the 2 alone.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know if an equation is linear?

The unknown has no power other than 1: no x², no square roots of x and no x in a denominator.

What if the equation has no solution?

That happens when the unknown cancels and leaves a false statement, such as 1 = 5. The calculator tells you.

Can I enter fractions and decimals?

Yes. Write fractions as 1/2 and decimals as 0.5.

Why does it show a check line?

It substitutes the answer back into the original equation so you can see that both sides agree.
