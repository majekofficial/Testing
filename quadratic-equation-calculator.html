Solving ax² + bx + c = 0 and understanding the parabola

Enter the three coefficients and the calculator returns the roots, the discriminant, the vertex, the axis of symmetry, the y-intercept, the vertex form and the factorised form, with a graph of the parabola. You can type whole numbers, decimals or fractions like 1/2. A box for b or c can be left empty to mean 0, but a cannot be 0.

The quadratic formula and the discriminant

x = (−b ± √(b² − 4ac)) ÷ 2a

The part under the root, b² − 4ac, is the discriminant. It tells you what kind of roots to expect before you solve anything:

  • Positive: two different real roots. The parabola crosses the x-axis twice.
  • Zero: one repeated root. The parabola just touches the x-axis.
  • Negative: no real roots. The parabola does not reach the x-axis and the roots are complex numbers.

Worked examples

Two whole-number roots

x² − 5x + 6 = 0. The discriminant is 25 − 24 = 1, so x = (5 ± 1) ÷ 2, giving x = 2 or x = 3. The factorised form is (x − 2)(x − 3) = 0. The vertex is at (5/2, −1/4) and the y-intercept at (0, 6).

A repeated root

x² + 2x + 1 = 0 has discriminant 4 − 4 = 0, so x = −1 only. The vertex is (−1, 0), exactly on the axis, and the factorised form is (x + 1)(x + 1).

Fractional roots

2x² + 3x − 5 = 0. The discriminant is 9 + 40 = 49, so x = (−3 ± 7) ÷ 4, which is x = 1 or x = −5/2. It factorises as (x − 1)(2x + 5).

Roots in surd form

x² − 2x − 1 = 0 has discriminant 4 + 4 = 8. The roots are x = 1 ± √2, which is about 2.4142 and −0.4142. Surds like this are explained on the Surd Calculator page.

No real roots

x² + 1 = 0 has discriminant −4. The calculator reports that the roots are complex numbers, x = ±i, and the parabola sits above the x-axis with its vertex at (0, 1).

Reading the other results

  • Vertex: the turning point, at x = −b ÷ 2a. It is a minimum when a is positive and a maximum when a is negative.
  • Axis of symmetry: the vertical line through the vertex.
  • Vertex form: y = a(x − h)² + k, where (h, k) is the vertex. It is handy for transformations and optimisation problems.
  • Roots and coefficients: the roots add up to −b ÷ a and multiply to c ÷ a. For x² − 5x + 6 these are 5 and 6.

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting to rearrange to “= 0” before reading off a, b and c.
  • Taking b as 5 instead of −5 when the equation is x² − 5x + 6. The sign belongs to the coefficient.
  • Putting a = 0. Then the equation is linear, and the calculator points you to the Linear Equation Calculator.
  • Dropping the ± and finding only one root.

Frequently asked questions

What is the discriminant?

It is b² − 4ac. Its sign decides whether the equation has two, one or no real roots.

Can the calculator handle decimals and fractions?

Yes. For example, enter 1/2 for a half. Empty b or c boxes count as 0.

Why does it say a cannot be 0?

If a is 0 the x² term disappears and the equation is linear, not quadratic.

What is the vertex form used for?

It shows the turning point directly, which helps when you need the maximum or minimum value or when you want to sketch the graph.
