What score do you need on the final exam?

Halfway through a course, the most useful question is not what your grade is, but what you still have to score to reach the grade you want. This calculator answers that. Tell it your current average, how much of the course that average already covers, and your target, and it returns the percentage you need on the remaining work.

The three inputs

  • Current grade (%) is your average over the work that has been marked so far.
  • Current work weight (%) is how much of the whole course that marked work represents. If coursework is worth 60% of the course and all of it is done, enter 60.
  • Target final grade (%) is the overall percentage you want when the course finishes.

The formula

needed score = (target − current grade × completed weight) ÷ remaining weight

Both weights are used as fractions of one, so 60% becomes 0.60 and the remaining work is 0.40. The numerator is the part of the target you have not yet earned, and dividing by the remaining weight tells you what average the unfinished work must reach to cover it.

Worked examples

A target within reach

Your average is 70% on work worth 60% of the course, and you want 75% overall.

(75 − 70 × 0.60) ÷ 0.40 = (75 − 42) ÷ 0.40 = 82.5%

You need 82.5% on the remaining 40%, which is realistic if you are working steadily.

A target that is out of reach

Same position, but you want 95%.

(95 − 42) ÷ 0.40 = 132.5%

Anything above 100% means the target cannot be reached unless extra credit is available. Choose a lower target or ask whether bonus marks exist.

Already almost there

Your average is 85% on 70% of the course and you only need 60%.

(60 − 85 × 0.70) ÷ 0.30 = 0.5 ÷ 0.30 = 1.67%

You would need less than 2% on the remaining work, so your pass is effectively secure.

A low start

A 62% average on 75% of the course and a target of 70%.

(70 − 62 × 0.75) ÷ 0.25 = 23.5 ÷ 0.25 = 94%

With only 25% of the course left, recovering is possible but demanding.

Common mistakes

  • Entering marks instead of weights. The weight field is a percentage of the course, not the number of marks you have so far.
  • Using a weight of 100. If the whole course is already marked, there is nothing left to change, and the calculator shows a dash.
  • Averaging uneven components. Make sure the current grade already reflects the weights of the pieces within it, for example by using a weighted average.
  • Several remaining assessments. The result is the average needed across everything left. If a test and an exam remain, you can split that requirement between them in any way that averages out to the number shown.

Frequently asked questions

Can the answer be more than 100%?

Yes, and it is a useful warning. It means the target cannot be reached through the remaining work alone.

Can the answer be below zero?

Yes. A negative or zero result means your current work already secures the target: even a 0 on the remaining work would leave you at or above it.

Does this work with letter grades?

No. Convert your grade to a percentage using your institution's scale, then enter that value.

What if my course has pass requirements on the exam itself?

Some courses require a minimum exam mark regardless of the overall average. This calculator only looks at the overall grade, so check your course rules.
