How much energy does it take to heat or melt something?

Heat energy is measured in joules. This calculator handles the two cases in school courses: energy to change the temperature of a material, and energy to change its state (for example, melting ice or boiling water) without changing its temperature.

The formulas

Q = m × c × ΔT (temperature change)

Q = m × L (change of state)

  • m is the mass in kilograms.
  • c is the specific heat capacity: the energy needed to raise 1 kg of the material by 1 °C. Water is about 4,200 J/(kg °C), aluminium about 900 and copper about 385.
  • ΔT is the temperature change in °C.
  • L is the specific latent heat, the energy to change the state of 1 kg. For ice melting it is about 334,000 J/kg and for water boiling about 2,260,000 J/kg.

These values are typical textbook figures, so use the ones given in your problem.

Worked examples

Warming water

How much energy raises 2 kg of water by 10 °C? Q = 2 × 4,200 × 10 = 84,000 J, or 84 kJ.

Melting ice

Melting 0.5 kg of ice at 0 °C takes Q = 0.5 × 334,000 = 167,000 J. The ice stays at 0 °C while it melts, because the energy goes into breaking bonds rather than raising the temperature.

Working backwards for c

If 84,000 J raises the temperature of a 2 kg sample by 10 °C, then c = 84,000 ÷ (2 × 10) = 4,200 J/(kg °C). That is how specific heat capacity is measured in an experiment.

Finding the temperature rise

Given 84,000 J, a mass of 2 kg and c = 4,200, the temperature change is 84,000 ÷ (2 × 4,200) = 10 °C.

Heating curves

Heating ice to steam happens in stages. First the temperature rises (Q = mcΔT), then it stays constant while the state changes (Q = mL), then it rises again, and so on. Calculate each stage separately and add the results. This tool does one stage at a time, so work through the stages in order.

Common mistakes

  • Using grams instead of kilograms.
  • Using the temperature change formula during a change of state.
  • Using the final temperature instead of the change.
  • Forgetting that real experiments lose energy to the surroundings, so the measured c is usually too high.

Frequently asked questions

What is specific heat capacity?

The energy needed to raise the temperature of 1 kg of a substance by 1 °C.

Why does the temperature stay constant during melting?

The energy supplied goes into changing the state of the material, not into raising its temperature.

Is a change of 1 °C the same as a change of 1 K?

Yes. Differences in degrees Celsius and kelvin are the same size.

Which is bigger, latent heat or specific heat?

For the same mass, the latent heat of boiling water is hundreds of times more energy than raising its temperature by one degree.
