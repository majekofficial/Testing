Describing a data set with a few numbers

Statistics condenses a list of values into measures of where the data is centred and how widely it is spread. Paste or type your numbers, separated by commas, spaces or new lines, and the calculator returns the mean, median and mode immediately, with a full set of statistics available under Show workings.

Centre: mean, median and mode

  • Mean: the sum divided by the count.
  • Median: the middle value when the numbers are sorted, or the average of the two middle values when there is an even number of them.
  • Mode: the value that appears most often. If every value is different, the calculator reports that there is no mode.

Spread: range, quartiles and standard deviation

  • Range: largest value minus smallest.
  • Quartiles: Q1 is the median of the lower half and Q3 the median of the upper half. For an odd count, the middle value is left out of both halves. The interquartile range (IQR) is Q3 − Q1.
  • Variance and standard deviation: shown both for a population (divide by n) and for a sample (divide by n − 1).

sample standard deviation = √( Σ(x − mean)² ÷ (n − 1) )

Worked examples

Five values

For 2, 4, 4, 6, 9:

  • sum = 25, mean = 5, median = 4, mode = 4, range = 7
  • Q1 = 3 and Q3 = 7.5, so IQR = 4.5
  • population variance 5.6 and standard deviation 2.3664
  • sample variance 7 and standard deviation 2.6458

The mean (5) is higher than the median (4) because the 9 pulls it upwards.

Eight values in a list

For 12 15 15 18 20 22 25 30 the mean is 19.625, the median is 19 (halfway between 18 and 20), the mode is 15 and the range is 18. Q1 is 15 and Q3 is 23.5, so the IQR is 8.5. The sample standard deviation is 5.9266.

No mode

For 5, 7, 9, 11, 13, 15 the mean and median are both 10. Because no value repeats, the result reads “No mode”. The IQR is 6.

Which measure should you use?

  • Use the median for skewed data such as incomes or house prices, where a few large values distort the mean.
  • Use the mean when values are fairly symmetric and every observation should count.
  • Use the sample standard deviation when your data is a sample from a larger group, and the population version only when you have every member.
  • Use the IQR to describe spread without being affected by extreme values.

Common mistakes

  • Typing a header or unit into the list.
  • Using the population standard deviation for a sample, which understates the spread.
  • Reporting the mean without looking at outliers.
  • Comparing quartiles from different software without checking the method. Different textbooks define quartiles slightly differently, which is why results can differ by a small amount.

Frequently asked questions

How do I enter the numbers?

Separate them with commas, spaces or new lines. You can paste a whole column from a spreadsheet.

What is the difference between sample and population standard deviation?

The sample version divides by n − 1 to correct for estimating from a subset. The population version divides by n and applies when you have all the data.

Can there be more than one mode?

Yes. If several values share the highest frequency, the data is multimodal. If all values are unique, there is no mode.

How are the quartiles calculated?

Each is the median of a half of the sorted data, excluding the middle value when the count is odd.
