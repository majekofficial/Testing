Trigonometry, logarithms, powers and factorials in one place

The scientific calculator adds the functions that school and college courses need: sine, cosine and tangent with their inverses, natural and base-10 logarithms, powers and roots, factorials, π and e. You can type the expression on your keyboard or use the keypad, and the answer updates as you go.

Degrees or radians

Trigonometric functions depend on the angle unit, and this is the most frequent reason for a wrong answer. The button labelled Deg (and Rad after you press it) switches the mode. The page starts in degrees.

  • In degrees, sin(30) = 0.5 and cos(60) = 0.5.
  • In radians, the same sin(30) is −0.988031624093, because 30 radians is not 30 degrees.
  • In radians, sin(π ÷ 6) = 0.5. In degrees, the same expression gives 0.00913839539718, because π ÷ 6 is only about 0.52 degrees.
  • Inverse functions return angles in the selected unit: asin(0.5) = 30 in degrees, and asin(1) = 1.57079632679 (that is π ÷ 2) in radians.

Worked examples

Order of operations with powers

√144 + 2⁵ = 12 + 32 = 44. Type sqrt(144)+2^5. Another: 2 × (3 + 4)² ÷ 7 = 2 × 49 ÷ 7 = 14.

Logarithms

log(1000) = 3 because 10 to the power 3 is 1000. ln(e) = 1 and ln(100) = 4.60517018599. Here log means base 10 and ln means base e.

Factorials

5! = 5 × 4 × 3 × 2 × 1 = 120 and 10! = 3,628,800. Factorials are the building block of permutations and combinations, which have their own tool.

An identity check

sin²(45°) + cos²(45°) should equal 1 for any angle. Typing sin(45)^2+cos(45)^2 in degree mode returns 1, a quick way to confirm the mode is set correctly.

Messages you may see

  • Tangent is undefined at this angle. tan(90°) has no value because cos(90°) = 0.
  • Logarithms need a number above 0. log(0) and logarithms of negative numbers do not exist among real numbers.
  • Cannot divide by zero. Any division by 0, including 1 ÷ 0.

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting to check Deg or Rad before using sin, cos or tan.
  • Confusing sin⁻¹ with 1 ÷ sin. The inverse button finds the angle; it does not take a reciprocal.
  • Leaving out a closing bracket on a function such as sin(30.
  • Using ln when the question asks for log, or the other way round.

For plain arithmetic, the Basic Calculator is simpler. To find a logarithm to any base, use the Logarithm Calculator, and for counting arrangements use the Permutation and Combination Calculator. Right-angled triangle problems also pair well with the Slope Calculator, since tan of an angle equals a gradient.

Frequently asked questions

Does it use degrees or radians?

Both. It starts in degrees and the toggle switches to radians and back. Check the label before you calculate.

Can I type π and e?

Yes. The keypad has π and e keys, and you can also use the constants in typed expressions such as sin(pi/6).

What does n! mean?

n factorial is the product of all whole numbers from 1 up to n. 5! is 120.

Why do I see a long decimal?

The calculator shows around twelve significant digits. That is more precise than most coursework requires, so round to the precision your question asks for.
