Density, pressure, liquid pressure and upthrust

Four related ideas appear together in school physics: how much mass is packed into a volume (density), how a force spreads over an area (pressure), how pressure builds with depth in a liquid and what holds objects up in water (upthrust). Choose a formula, choose what to find and enter the other values.

The formulas

  • Density: ρ = m ÷ V, in kg/m³.
  • Pressure: P = F ÷ A, in pascals (Pa), where 1 Pa is 1 N per square metre.
  • Pressure in a liquid: P = ρgh, where h is the depth below the surface.
  • Upthrust (Archimedes' principle): U = ρgV, the weight of the fluid displaced.

Worked examples

Density

A block with mass 2 kg and volume 0.001 m³ (one litre) has density 2 ÷ 0.001 = 2,000 kg/m³. To find mass instead, use m = ρV: an aluminium block of density 2,700 kg/m³ and volume 0.004 m³ has a mass of 10.8 kg.

Pressure

A force of 500 N spread over 0.25 m² gives 500 ÷ 0.25 = 2,000 Pa. Reducing the area increases the pressure for the same force, which is why a sharp knife cuts and a wide snowshoe does not sink.

Pressure under water

At 10 m depth in fresh water (1,000 kg/m³) and g = 9.8, P = 1,000 × 9.8 × 10 = 98,000 Pa. This is the pressure from the water alone; atmospheric pressure at the surface, about 101,000 Pa, adds to it in real life.

Upthrust and floating

A fully submerged object of volume 0.002 m³ in water experiences an upthrust U = 1,000 × 9.8 × 0.002 = 19.6 N. It floats if its weight is less than the upthrust, and sinks if it is greater.

Reading the results

  • Density comparisons: an object floats in a fluid when its average density is less than the fluid's. Fresh water is about 1,000 kg/m³, so ice, at about 920 kg/m³, floats.
  • Liquid pressure depends on depth and density, not on the width or shape of the container.
  • Units: use kilograms, metres and newtons. A volume of 1 litre is 0.001 m³ and 1 g/cm³ is 1,000 kg/m³.

Common mistakes

  • Entering volume in litres or cm³ when the formula expects m³.
  • Forgetting g in liquid pressure and upthrust, or using a mass instead of a density.
  • Confusing the volume of the whole object with the volume submerged. Upthrust depends only on the submerged part.
  • Treating pressure as a force. Pressure is force per area.

Frequently asked questions

Why does pressure increase with depth?

The deeper you go, the more liquid is above you, and the weight of that liquid presses down on you.

What is Archimedes' principle?

The upthrust on an object in a fluid equals the weight of the fluid that the object displaces.

How do I convert g/cm³ to kg/m³?

Multiply by 1,000. Water at 1 g/cm³ is 1,000 kg/m³.

What value of g is used?
