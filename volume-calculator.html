Volume and surface area of eight solids

Choose a solid, pick the length unit and enter its dimensions. The calculator returns the volume and, for most shapes, the surface area, and it also converts the volume to cubic metres, litres and cubic feet. The eight solids are cube, cuboid, cylinder, cone, sphere, hemisphere, rectangular pyramid and triangular prism.

The volume formulas

  • Cube: a³. Cuboid: l × w × h.
  • Cylinder: πr²h. Cone: ⅓πr²h. Sphere: ⁴⁄₃πr³. Hemisphere: ⅔πr³.
  • Rectangular pyramid: ⅓ × l × w × h.
  • Triangular prism: ½ × base × triangle height × prism length.

A useful pattern: a cone is one third of the cylinder with the same base and height, and a pyramid is one third of the prism with the same base and height.

Worked examples (all in centimetres)

Cube and cuboid

A cube of side 3: volume 27 cm³, surface area 54 cm², which is 0.027 litres. A cuboid 5 × 4 × 3 has volume 60 cm³ and surface area 94 cm².

A cylinder

Radius 3 and height 10: volume = π × 9 × 10 = 282.7433 cm³. The curved surface area is 2πrh = 188.4956 cm² and the total surface area, including both ends, is 245.0442 cm². That is 0.2827 litres, roughly a small can.

A cone

Radius 3 and height 10: volume = ⅓ × π × 9 × 10 = 94.2478 cm³. The slant height is √(3² + 10²) = 10.4403 cm and the total surface area is 126.6719 cm².

A sphere and a hemisphere

Radius 6: the sphere has volume 904.7787 cm³ and surface area 452.3893 cm². The hemisphere holds half, 452.3893 cm³, and its total surface area, including the flat circle, is 339.292 cm².

A pyramid and a prism

A pyramid with a 6 × 4 base and height 9 has volume ⅓ × 24 × 9 = 72 cm³. A triangular prism with triangle base 6, triangle height 4 and length 10 has volume ½ × 6 × 4 × 10 = 120 cm³. Surface area is not shown for these two.

Capacity and real-world conversions

1,000 cm³ equals 1 litre, and 1 m³ equals 1,000 litres. The result panel shows litres directly, which is useful for tanks and containers. When you work with mixed units, convert lengths first with the Unit Converter, because volume units scale with the cube of the length factor.

Common mistakes

  • Using the slant height of a cone or pyramid in the volume formula. Use the perpendicular height.
  • Entering the diameter in the radius field.
  • Mixing units, such as a radius in centimetres and a height in metres.
  • Forgetting that cubic units scale by the cube: 1 m³ is 1,000,000 cm³, not 100 or 1,000.

The base of a cylinder or cone is a circle, so the Area Calculator gives its area. For the mass of material that fills a solid, combine volume with density using the Density and Pressure Calculator.

Frequently asked questions

How do I find the volume of a pipe?

Treat it as a cylinder. Use πr²h with the inside radius and the length of the pipe.

Why is a cone one third of a cylinder?

It is a geometric result: a cone fills exactly one third of a cylinder with the same base and height.

How many litres are in a cubic metre?

1,000. The calculator shows the litres for you.

Why is no surface area shown for the pyramid and prism?

This tool gives volume only for these two shapes, so use a separate surface-area calculation if you need it.
