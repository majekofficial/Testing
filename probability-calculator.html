Calculate the probability of an event

Probability describes how likely an event is to happen. This calculator finds the probability when you know the number of favourable outcomes and the total number of equally likely possible outcomes. It displays the answer as a fraction, decimal and percentage, together with the probability that the event will not happen.

How to use the calculator

  1. Count the outcomes that match the event you are interested in.
  2. Enter that count as the favourable outcomes.
  3. Enter the total number of possible outcomes.
  4. Calculate and read the probability in the result panel.

Probability formula

For equally likely outcomes, P(A) = number of favourable outcomes ÷ total number of possible outcomes. The probability is always between 0 and 1 inclusive. A probability of 0 means the event is impossible in the model, while 1 means it is certain.

Worked examples

Rolling a fair die

A standard die has six equally likely faces. The probability of rolling a 4 is 1 ÷ 6 = 0.1667, or about 16.67%. Enter 1 as favourable outcomes and 6 as total outcomes.

Picking a red ball

A bag contains 3 red balls and 7 blue balls. If one ball is selected at random, the probability of selecting red is 3 ÷ 10 = 0.3 = 30%. The probability of not selecting red is 70%.

Getting an even number

On a fair six-sided die, the even outcomes are 2, 4 and 6. There are three favourable outcomes out of six, so P(even) = 3/6 = 1/2 = 50%.

Understanding the result formats

A fraction shows the favourable share of all possible outcomes. A decimal expresses the same value on a scale from 0 to 1. A percentage multiplies the decimal by 100. For example, 1/4, 0.25 and 25% all describe the same probability. Odds in favour compare favourable outcomes with outcomes that are not favourable; odds are not the same as probability.

Assumptions and limitations

  • The simple formula assumes every possible outcome is equally likely.
  • Enter whole-number counts, not percentages or decimal probabilities.
  • This tool calculates one event from a single set of outcomes. It does not calculate conditional probability or combined events such as A or B.
  • Real-life probability estimates may require data, assumptions or statistical models that are not represented by a simple outcome count.

Frequently asked questions

Can probability be greater than 1?

No. A valid probability is from 0 to 1, or from 0% to 100%.

What is the difference between odds and probability?

Probability compares favourable outcomes with all outcomes. Odds in favour compare favourable outcomes with unfavourable outcomes.

What if all outcomes are favourable?

If the favourable count equals the total count, the probability is 1 or 100%.
