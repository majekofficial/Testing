Use Ohm’s law to calculate an electrical circuit

This electric circuit calculator uses Ohm’s law and the electrical power relationship to find voltage, current, resistance and power. Enter any two known quantities and leave the other two fields blank. The calculator derives the remaining values for a simple ideal DC circuit.

How to use the calculator

  1. Enter exactly two known values among voltage, current, resistance and power.
  2. Leave the other two fields empty.
  3. Calculate to see all four quantities and the relationships used.

Formulas

  • Ohm’s law: V = I × R.
  • Electrical power: P = V × I.
  • Using substitutions: P = I²R and P = V²/R.

Voltage is measured in volts (V), current in amperes (A), resistance in ohms (Ω), and power in watts (W).

Worked example

A simple circuit has a voltage of 12 V and a resistance of 4 Ω. Current is I = V/R = 12/4 = 3 A. Power is P = V × I = 12 × 3 = 36 W. Enter 12 in voltage and 4 in resistance to reproduce these results.

Another example using power

If a component has a resistance of 10 Ω and dissipates 40 W, current is I = √(P/R) = √(40/10) = 2 A. Voltage is V = IR = 2 × 10 = 20 V.

Safety and limitations

  • This calculator is for learning and simple ideal DC circuit calculations.
  • It does not model AC impedance, phase, frequency, transformers or complex multi-branch networks.
  • Real components have tolerances and power ratings. A calculated value does not confirm that a component is safe to use.
  • Never experiment with mains electricity. Practical electrical work should be carried out by a qualified person using appropriate safety procedures.

Frequently asked questions

Can I enter three known values?

This version asks for exactly two values so the calculation path remains clear and consistent. Leave the other two fields blank.

What is the difference between voltage and current?

Voltage is the electrical potential difference between two points. Current is the rate at which electric charge flows.

What happens if I know resistance and power?

The calculator uses I = √(P/R) to find current, then V = IR to find voltage.
