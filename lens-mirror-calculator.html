Focal length, image distance and magnification

Lenses and curved mirrors form images, and where the image appears depends on how far the object is from the lens and on the focal length. This calculator uses the lens and mirror formula to find any one of the focal length, object distance and image distance, and the magnification formula to compare image and object sizes.

The formulas

1 ÷ f = 1 ÷ u + 1 ÷ v

magnification m = v ÷ u

  • f is the focal length, u the object distance and v the image distance, all measured from the lens or mirror.
  • This tool uses the “real is positive” convention: distances to real objects and real images are positive. A negative result indicates a virtual image or object, which is on the other side.
  • Magnification is the ratio of image size to object size. A value below 1 means a smaller image, above 1 a larger one.

Worked examples

Finding the focal length

An object 30 cm from a lens forms an image 15 cm from it. Then 1 ÷ f = 1/30 + 1/15 = 3/30 = 1/10, so f = 10 cm.

Finding the image distance

With f = 10 cm and an object at 30 cm, 1 ÷ v = 1/10 − 1/30 = 2/30 = 1/15, so v = 15 cm. With the object at 15 cm, 1 ÷ v = 1/10 − 1/15 = 1/30, so v = 30 cm. Bringing the object closer moves the image further away.

Magnification

For an image distance of 15 cm and an object distance of 30 cm, m = 15 ÷ 30 = 0.5: the image is half the size of the object.

An object at twice the focal length

If u = 10 cm and v = 10 cm, f = 5 cm. The object is at 2f, the image is at 2f and the magnification is 1: the image is the same size as the object.

A negative result

With f = 30 cm and an image distance of 10 cm, 1 ÷ u = 1/30 − 1/10 = −1/15, so u = −15 cm. A negative object distance means the numbers describe a virtual object, which is unusual in school problems. A real object in front of a converging lens cannot give a real image closer than the focal length, so if you expected a real object, recheck your values.

Where the image appears

  • Beyond 2f: a smaller, real image between f and 2f. This is how a camera works.
  • At 2f: a real image of the same size at 2f.
  • Between f and 2f: a larger real image beyond 2f, as in a projector.
  • Inside f: a virtual, magnified image on the same side as the object, as with a magnifying glass.

Common mistakes

  • Mixing up sign conventions between textbooks. Some count distances to virtual images as negative and others use the Cartesian convention. Check which one your course uses.
  • Measuring distances from the wrong point on a thick lens.
  • Using the formula for a thin lens with a thick or very curved one.
  • Treating magnification as a length instead of a ratio.

Frequently asked questions

What is a focal length?

The distance from a lens or mirror to the point where parallel rays meet after passing through or reflecting from it.

What does a negative image distance mean?

In this convention, the image is virtual, on the same side as the object, and cannot be projected onto a screen.

What is the difference between a real and a virtual image?

A real image is formed where light actually converges and can be shown on a screen. A virtual image only appears to come from a point.

Does the formula work for both lenses and mirrors?

Yes. The same relationship applies to thin lenses and to curved mirrors with small apertures.
