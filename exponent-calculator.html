Powers, roots and negative exponents

An exponent tells you how many times to multiply a base by itself. This calculator evaluates a base raised to a whole, negative, decimal or fractional exponent, and it shows exact fractions where it can alongside the decimal value.

The rules to remember

  • Positive whole exponent: 210 = 2 × 2 × … (ten times) = 1,024.
  • Zero exponent: any non-zero number to the power 0 is 1, so 50 = 1.
  • Negative exponent: a reciprocal. 10−3 = 1 ÷ 1,000 = 0.001.
  • Fractional exponent: a root. 91/2 is the square root of 9, and 272/3 is the cube root of 27 squared.

am × an = am+n | (am)n = amn | a−n = 1 ÷ an

Worked examples

Whole and negative powers

2 to the power 10 is 1,024. (−2) to the power 3 is −8: an odd power keeps the negative sign, while an even power would make it positive. 2 to the power −1 is 1/2.

Roots as exponents

9 to the power 1/2 is 3, and 27 to the power 2/3 is 9, because the cube root of 27 is 3 and 3² = 9. 2 to the power 0.5 is the square root of 2, about 1.41421356.

Compound growth

Money growing at 5% a year multiplies by 1.05 each year. After 10 years the factor is 1.0510. The calculator treats 1.05 as the fraction 21/20 and returns about 1.62889463, so 1,000 would grow to roughly 1,628.89.

Powers of 10

10 to the power −3 is 1/1000, which is 0.001. Powers of 10 are the basis of standard form and unit prefixes.

What the calculator will not return

  • A negative base with a fractional exponent, such as (−8)1/3, shows a dash. The real cube root of −8 is −2, but the tool does not handle roots of negative bases, so compute that case by hand.
  • 0 to the power 0 shows a dash because it is undefined or ambiguous depending on context.
  • Fractions are entered as a/b, for example 1/2.

Common mistakes

  • Reading −2² as 4. The usual convention treats it as −(2²) = −4, while (−2)² = 4. Enter the base on its own to avoid confusion.
  • Adding exponents when bases differ. 2³ × 3² is not 6⁵.
  • Treating a negative exponent as a negative result. 2⁻¹ is 0.5, which is positive.

Exponents are the inverse of logarithms, so for “what power gives this number” use the Logarithm Calculator. For square roots in exact form, the Surd Calculator simplifies them. Powers also appear in the Sequence Calculator (geometric sequences) and in the Scientific Calculator via the xʸ key.

Frequently asked questions

What is anything to the power 0?

Any non-zero number to the power 0 equals 1. The expression 0⁰ is not defined here.

How do I calculate a square root?

Use the exponent 1/2. The square root of 49 is 49 to the power 1/2, which is 7.

Why does the calculator show a fraction?

Exact results are shown as fractions when the numbers allow it, with a decimal approximation alongside.

Why do I see a dash for (−8) to the power 1/3?

The tool does not evaluate fractional powers of negative bases. The cube root of −8 is −2 by hand.
