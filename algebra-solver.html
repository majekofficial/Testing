Simplify, factorise or solve: one box for algebra

Type an algebraic expression and the solver expands and simplifies it, then factorises it. Add an equals sign and it solves the equation for the letter you choose. You can write powers as x^2 or x², use brackets and fractions such as 1/2, and place letters next to each other to multiply them, so 5xy means 5 × x × y.

What happens to an expression

Without an equals sign you get two forms. The expanded and simplified form multiplies out brackets and collects like terms. The factorised form writes the result as a product, which is useful for solving and for cancelling fractions.

  • 2x + 3x − 4 + 9 simplifies to 5x + 5, which factorises as 5(x + 1).
  • (x + 2)(x + 3) expands to x² + 5x + 6, and the factorised form shows the brackets again.
  • x² − 5x + 6 factorises to (x − 2)(x − 3), because 2 and 3 multiply to 6 and add to 5.
  • x³ − 8 factorises as (x − 2)(x² + 2x + 4), an example of the difference of two cubes.

What happens to an equation

With an equals sign, choose the letter to solve for. Linear, quadratic and cubic equations in one letter are solved, and equations with several letters are rearranged for the one you pick.

  • 3x + 5 = 20 gives x = 5.
  • 2(x + 4) = 3x − 1 gives x = 9: expanding gives 2x + 8 = 3x − 1, so x = 9.
  • x² − 9 = 0 gives x = −3 or x = 3.
  • 2x³ − 3x² − 11x + 6 = 0 gives x = −2, x = 1/2 or x = 3.
  • 5x + 3y − 6 = 0 solved for x gives x = (6 − 3y) ÷ 5.
  • x² + 1 = 0 has no real solutions. The solver says so and shows the complex roots x = ±i.

Checking your own working

Use the solver as an answer key after you have tried a problem. Compare each stage of your working with the shown steps rather than only the final line. A wrong sign when you move a term across the equals sign is the most common slip, and the steps show exactly where it occurs. You can also substitute your answer back into the original equation: both sides should match.

Common mistakes

  • Writing x2 instead of x^2, which reads as x × 2.
  • Forgetting to multiply every term inside a bracket: 2(x + 4) is 2x + 8, not 2x + 4.
  • Dividing both sides by a letter that could be zero, which loses solutions. Factorising avoids that.
  • Entering two equations at once. Use the Simultaneous Equations Calculator for systems.

For a single linear equation with a worked layout, see the Linear Equation Calculator. Quadratics have their own page with vertex and graph in the Quadratic Equation Calculator, and the Change of Subject Calculator rearranges formulas.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between expanding and factorising?

Expanding multiplies brackets out into a sum of terms. Factorising does the reverse and writes a sum as a product.

Can it solve equations with more than one letter?

Yes, for the letter you select. 5x + 3y − 6 = 0 solved for x gives x = (6 − 3y) ÷ 5.

What does “complex roots” mean?

The equation has no solution among real numbers. Its solutions involve i, the square root of −1.

Can I check whether my answer is right?

Substitute it into the original equation. The sides must be equal. The solver also shows a step-by-step working.
