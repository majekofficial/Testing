Determinant, inverse, transpose and adjugate of a matrix

Choose a 2 × 2 or 3 × 3 matrix, enter its entries and pick the operation. The calculator returns the result and can show the working. Whole numbers, decimals and fractions such as 1/2 are accepted in every cell.

The four operations

Determinant

2 × 2: det [[a, b], [c, d]] = ad − bc

The determinant is a single number. A matrix has an inverse only if its determinant is not 0. For [[1, 2], [3, 4]], det = 1 × 4 − 2 × 3 = −2, and for [[3, 4], [8, 6]], det = 18 − 32 = −14.

Inverse

A⁻¹ = (1 ÷ det A) × adjugate of A

The inverse undoes the matrix: multiplying a matrix by its inverse gives the identity matrix. For [[4, 7], [2, 6]], det = 24 − 14 = 10, so the inverse is [[3/5, −7/10], [−1/5, 2/5]].

Transpose

The transpose swaps rows and columns. The transpose of [[1, 2], [3, 4]] is [[1, 3], [2, 4]]. For a 3 × 3 matrix, the entry in row 1, column 2 moves to row 2, column 1.

Adjugate

For a 2 × 2 matrix [[a, b], [c, d]], the adjugate is [[d, −b], [−c, a]]. For [[1, 2], [3, 4]] it is [[4, −2], [−3, 1]]. For 3 × 3 matrices the adjugate is the transpose of the matrix of cofactors.

A 3 × 3 example

For the matrix with rows (2, 0, 1), (1, 3, 2) and (1, 1, 4), the determinant is 2(12 − 2) − 0 + 1(1 − 3) = 20 − 2 = 18. Because it is not 0, the inverse exists and equals one eighteenth of the adjugate. The transpose of the 3 × 3 matrix with rows (1, 2, 3), (4, 5, 6) and (7, 8, 9) has rows (1, 4, 7), (2, 5, 8) and (3, 6, 9).

Singular matrices

If the determinant is 0, the matrix is singular and has no inverse. [[1, 2], [2, 4]] is an example, because its second row is twice the first. The calculator tells you that the matrix has no inverse because its determinant is 0. The matrix with rows (2, 0, 1), (1, 3, 2), (1, 1, 1) also has determinant 0.

Common mistakes

  • Mixing up the order of rows and columns when entering entries. The labels read Row · Column.
  • Forgetting the minus sign in the 2 × 2 adjugate.
  • Trying to invert a matrix with determinant 0.
  • Treating the determinant formula for 2 × 2 as if it applied to 3 × 3 matrices.

Frequently asked questions

What does a determinant of 0 mean?

The matrix is singular: it has no inverse, and its rows or columns are dependent.

How do I find an inverse by hand?

Compute the determinant and the adjugate, then divide each entry of the adjugate by the determinant.

Can I enter fractions?

Yes, as 1/2 for example. Results are shown as exact fractions.

What is the identity matrix?

A matrix with 1s on the main diagonal and 0s elsewhere. Multiplying by it leaves a matrix unchanged.
