Finding the time between two clock times

Shift lengths, study sessions, travel times and meeting durations all start with the same question: how many hours and minutes lie between a start and an end? Enter both times and this calculator returns the elapsed time as hours and minutes.

How it works

duration = end time − start time

If the end time is earlier than the start time, the calculator treats it as the next day, so a shift from 23:15 to 06:40 is counted across midnight. If the two times are identical the result is 0h 0m, not 24 hours.

Worked examples

A working day

08:45 to 17:20.

17:20 − 08:45 = 8 hours 35 minutes

Across midnight

23:15 to 06:40 is 45 minutes to midnight plus 6 hours 40 minutes: 7 hours 25 minutes.

A short meeting

12:00 to 12:45 is 0 hours 45 minutes.

Subtracting a break

The calculator finds the full span, so take breaks off by hand. If the working day above includes a 45-minute lunch, 8h 35m − 45m = 7h 50m.

Turning the answer into pay or decimals

Payroll often needs decimal hours. Divide the minutes by 60: 7 hours 50 minutes is 7 + 50 ÷ 60 = 7.83 hours. Multiply by the hourly rate to estimate pay. If you need to convert a yearly salary into an hourly amount, start with the Salary Calculator.

Limits

  • Same-day and overnight only. The result is always under 24 hours. For longer spans, use the Date Calculator for the days.
  • No time zones or daylight-saving changes. If a clock change falls between the times, the real elapsed time may differ by an hour.
  • Clock format. You may see 12-hour or 24-hour entry depending on your device. Check am and pm carefully.

Common mistakes

  • Mixing up am and pm, which can add or remove 12 hours.
  • Forgetting that an equal start and end time shows zero, not a full day.
  • Counting unpaid breaks as working time.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate time across midnight?

Enter the start time and the end time on the next day. The calculator treats an earlier end time as the following day automatically.

Can I enter seconds?

No. The tool works in hours and minutes.

Does it subtract breaks?

No. Subtract any break yourself after you have the total span.

Why is the result 0h 0m?

The start and end times are the same. For a full 24 hours, use the Date Calculator to count the days.
