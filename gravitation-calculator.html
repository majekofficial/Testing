Newton's law of universal gravitation

Every mass attracts every other mass. The force is tiny for everyday objects and enormous for planets, which is why gravity governs the Moon's orbit but is not noticeable between two people. The calculator covers the force between two masses and the gravitational field strength at a distance from a planet or star.

The formulas

F = G × m₁ × m₂ ÷ r²

g = G × M ÷ r²

G is the gravitational constant, 6.674 × 10⁻¹¹ N m²/kg², and r is the distance between the centres of the masses. Field strength g is the force per kilogram, in N/kg, which is equivalent to the acceleration of a free-falling object.

Worked examples

Two small masses

Masses of 10 kg and 20 kg whose centres are 2 m apart: F = 6.674 × 10⁻¹¹ × 10 × 20 ÷ 4 = 3.337 × 10⁻⁹ N. That is about three billionths of a newton, far too small to feel.

The inverse-square law

Doubling the distance cuts the force to a quarter. With the same two masses at 4 m, F = 8.3425 × 10⁻¹⁰ N, a quarter of the previous value. Halving the distance would multiply it by four.

Gravity at the Earth's surface

Using the Earth's mass 5.972 × 10²⁴ kg and radius 6.371 × 10⁶ m, g = 6.674 × 10⁻¹¹ × 5.972 × 10²⁴ ÷ (6.371 × 10⁶)² = 9.81953 N/kg. That is the familiar 9.8 m/s² within rounding.

The Earth and the Moon

With masses 5.972 × 10²⁴ kg and 7.35 × 10²² kg at a distance of 3.844 × 10⁸ m, F = 1.9826 × 10²⁰ N. Enter large numbers in scientific notation, such as 5.972e24.

Working with large and small numbers

  • Type 5.972e24 for 5.972 × 10²⁴ and 6.371e6 for 6.371 × 10⁶.
  • Distances are measured between centres, so use the planet's radius plus any height above the surface.
  • Choose the standard G unless your course gives a different value.
  • The solver can also find G, a mass or the distance, when you know the force.

Common mistakes

  • Using the distance from the surface instead of the distance from the centre.
  • Forgetting to square the distance.
  • Mixing kilometres and metres.
  • Confusing g, the field strength, with G, the universal constant.

Frequently asked questions

Why is gravity so weak between everyday objects?

G is a very small number, so large masses are needed before the force is noticeable.

What is the difference between g and G?

G is the universal gravitational constant. g is the field strength at a particular place, such as about 9.8 N/kg on Earth's surface.

Does the force act on both masses?

Yes. Both masses feel an equal and opposite force, as Newton's third law requires.

Can I use miles or kilometres?

Convert to metres first. The calculator works in SI units.
